About this design

Deep bottle green with a soft sheen, inside a dashed gold inner border. A gold rope swings in loops across the top, and from it hang nine parandi tassels on beaded gold cords, each in gold with red and green threads, like the braid ornaments worn on the night. A woven band in deep red with small gold motifs runs along the bottom edge.

The Punjabi text opens with ੴ ਸਤਿ ਨਾਮੁ and an Ek Onkar emblem in a gold ring, then ਮਹਿੰਦੀ ਦੀ ਰਸਮ in large gold letters. The invitation line asks guests to come to the colourful Mehendi evening, and the names Simran Kaur and Harpreet Singh are in bold gold Gurmukhi with ਸੰਗ between them. The date, time, venue and a ਦਰਸ਼ਨ ਅਭਿਲਾਖੀ block with two names and a phone number follow, all in gold.

Mayun is the Punjabi pre-wedding gathering, often on the same night as the Mehendi, and many families send one card for both. Two sample lines need checking before you send it. The time reads ਦੁਪਹਿਰ 3:00 ਵਜੇ, three in the afternoon, while the invitation line calls it an evening, so make the two agree. The venue is a gurdwara in Ludhiana, carried over from the Punjabi wedding sample; for a Mehendi at home, put the house name and area instead. If you are combining Mayun, Mehendi and Jaago into one evening, name all of them in the heading. Compared with Dhol Jaago Phulkari, the Punjabi Sangeet card in maroon with a dhol, Mayun Night is cooler in colour and quieter. Misri Roka Phulkari and Anand Karaj Royal Blue complete a Punjabi set from the engagement to the wedding, both opening with Ek Onkar.
