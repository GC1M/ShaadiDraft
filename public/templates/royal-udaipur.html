About this design

A deep maroon card, lightest at the upper centre and close to black at the corners, framed by a tall gold arch with a pointed top. Strings of orange and yellow marigolds with green leaves hang across the top as a toran. Along the bottom runs a palace skyline in gold line on darker maroon: a central domed pavilion, four smaller chhatris and a long row of arches, standing over water marked with gold ripples, after the lake palaces of Udaipur.

All the text is in gold and cream. It opens with || Shri Ganeshaya Namah ||, the Vakratunda shlok written in English letters, and an Om emblem, then Shubh Vivah in large Pinyon Script. The names Ananya and Arjun are in the same gold script with D/o and S/o lines, the date is in Cinzel capitals, and the baraat and pheras times, the venue and the RSVP follow.

It is the most formal English card in the wedding set and runs to the full six pages: the functions (a mandap muhurat, a garba night and a note from the children), the wedding-day programme, the letter, the family names and a closing message. That suits Hindu families who want one English card that guests from other states and from abroad can read, whether the wedding is at a palace hotel or a city banquet hall.

If most of your guests read Hindi, Shubh Vivah Traditional has the same programme in Hindi on a lighter saffron card. For a Telugu or South Indian family, Pelli Patrika Turmeric Mandapam is also in English, with a Venkateswara invocation.
