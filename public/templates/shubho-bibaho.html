About this design

Sindoor red, darker at the edges, with a white alpana lace border of loops and dots running all the way round, the rice-paste patterns drawn at Bengali weddings, and a small white alpana rosette in each corner. A thin gold line frames the text. At the bottom stands the one hero image, a tall white topor, the groom's sola-pith crown, outlined in gold with a red dot at its centre and a fan of white outlined leaves behind it. The border and topor repeat on all six pages.

The English cover opens with || Shri Ganeshaya Namah || in gold and the Vakratunda shlok in small cream type, then an Om emblem in a gold ring and Shubho Bibaho in large cream Great Vibes script. The parents' invitation line is in serif, the names Ishita and Sayan are in gold script with D/o and S/o lines, and the date is in gold Cinzel capitals, followed by the times, the venue and the RSVP.

It is made for Bengali families who want an English card that still looks Bengali, and the sample wording follows a Bengali wedding. The cover gives the lagna time and dinner, and the functions page lists the Aiburobhaat, the bride's farewell feast two evenings before, and the Gaye Holud on the wedding morning with turmeric sent from the groom's home. The wedding-night page has the Bor Jatri arrival, the lagna with Shubho Drishti, Mala Badal and Sampradan, and dinner. The letter is headed Amontron, and the family page includes the Mamar Bari, the maternal uncle's side. Change the names, times and the Kolkata banquet hall to your own; if your family holds the Bou Bhat as a separate reception, it can go on its own card.

Compared with Royal Udaipur Maroon and Gold, the other English wedding card that opens with Shri Ganeshaya Namah and the shlok, Shubho Bibaho has no toran or palace and relies on the red and white alpana and the topor. Kerala Kasavu Gold Border does the same job for Malayali families, and Shubh Vivah Traditional is the Hindi card for North Indian families.
