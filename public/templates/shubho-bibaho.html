About this design

Sindoor red, darker at the edges, with a white alpana lace border of loops and dots running all the way round, the rice-paste patterns drawn at Bengali weddings, and a small white alpana rosette in each corner. A thin gold line frames the text. At the bottom stands the one hero image, a tall white topor, the groom's sola-pith crown, outlined in gold with a red dot at its centre and a fan of white outlined leaves behind it. The border and topor repeat on all six pages.

The English cover opens with || Shri Ganeshaya Namah || in gold and the Vakratunda shlok in small cream type, then an Om emblem in a gold ring and Shubh Vivah in large cream Great Vibes script. The parents' invitation line is in serif, the names Ananya and Arjun are in gold script with D/o and S/o lines, and the date is in gold Cinzel capitals, followed by the times, the venue and the RSVP.

It is made for Bengali families who want an English card that still looks Bengali, but the sample wording is the shared English Hindu wedding text, so several lines need changing. On the cover, the time line reads Baraat at 6:00 PM | Hast Melap at 7:30 PM; rename these to the events and times your family is holding, and you may want the heading to read Shubho Bibaho rather than Shubh Vivah. On page 2, the sample functions are Mandap Muhurat and Garba Night, so replace both with your own. On page 3, change Baraat Arrival, Hast Melap and Pheras under the mandap to your own programme. The names, the family list and the Udaipur hotel are placeholders too.

Compared with Royal Udaipur Maroon and Gold, the other English wedding card that opens with Shri Ganeshaya Namah and the shlok, Shubho Bibaho has no toran or palace and relies on the red and white alpana and the topor. Kerala Kasavu Gold Border does the same job for Malayali families, and Shubh Vivah Traditional is the Hindi card for North Indian families.
