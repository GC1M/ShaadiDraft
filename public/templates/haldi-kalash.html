About this design

Burnt saffron, glowing brighter at the centre and darker at the edges, with a gold line around the card and a fine zigzag just inside it. A string of round marigolds runs in loose swags across the top, with pairs of mango leaves hanging from it. At the bottom stands a large brass kalash with a red band and a small round medallion on its belly, a coconut on mango leaves at its mouth and fine gold rays behind it. A lit diya sits in each lower corner.

The text opens with ॥ श्री गणेशाय नमः ॥ and a gold Om emblem. हल्दी रस्म is set large in gold, followed by a one-line invitation to the Haldi. The names are in cream with चि. before each, joined by संग, and the date, a 10 am start, the venue and the निवेदक block follow in gold and cream. The numerals are in Latin digits, and the venue is a placeholder hotel in Udaipur.

It suits North Indian families who want the Haldi card to match the colour of the day and keep the Ganesha line of the wedding card. The sample names both the bride and the groom; if your Haldi is for one side only, keep one name and change the venue to your home. Compared with Marigold & Haldi Sunshine, the English Haldi card with no religious line, Haldi Kalash is darker and more traditional, and the kalash and diyas give it the feel of a puja where Sunshine is all flowers. Marathi families can use Rangoli Haldi, and Tamil families Mangala Snanam Turmeric Bath, which has a brass kalash too.
