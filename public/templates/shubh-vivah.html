About this design

Cream in the centre, glowing out to saffron at the edges. A toran of hanging marigold strings in orange and yellow, with green leaves between them and small red drops at the tips, runs along the top edge. At the bottom, a half mandala of orange and olive petals rises from the edge, a brass kalash topped with a coconut and mango leaves stands in each corner, and five small diyas burn in a row at the centre.

The text is laid out the way a printed North Indian card is: ॥ श्री गणेशाय नमः ॥, the Vakratunda shlok in two small lines, an Om emblem, then शुभ विवाह in large red letters. The names चि. अनन्या and चि. अर्जुन are in maroon Amita script, joined by संग, with सुपुत्री and सुपुत्र lines beneath. The date, the baraat and phere times, the venue and the निवेदक block follow in Tiro Devanagari.

The inner pages stay in the same Hindi: मांगलिक अवसर for the mandap muhurat and other functions, सुनहरे पल for the baraat, hast milap and dinner, a परिणयोत्सव letter, the दर्शनाभिलाषी list of relatives, and a closing note asking guests to reply OK.

It suits Hindi-speaking families across North and Central India, for a banquet hall, a hotel lawn or a wedding at home. Compared with Royal Udaipur Maroon & Gold, which has the same programme in English on a dark maroon card, Shubh Vivah is lighter and reads more like a traditional printed card. For a Hindi card with a different look, see Kashmiri Chinar & Shikara.
