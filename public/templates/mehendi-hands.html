About this design

Terracotta, the colour of fresh henna paste, a shade darker at the top and bottom. A narrow lace border of copper scallops and dots runs along the top edge. A thin copper line draws a slim onion arch that rises to a point with a small dot above it and runs down both sides to the bottom. In the lower corners two hennaed hands rise, palms towards you, with dark fingertips and a round mandala on each palm.

A small copper ring with a lotus sits inside the top of the arch. Mehendi Ceremony is in cream Pinyon Script, the names Ananya and Arjun are in pale peach script, and the invitation line, date, time, venue and RSVP are in a plain serif in cream and peach. There is no invocation or religious line. The sample asks guests to an afternoon of henna, music and colour from 3:00 pm, and the venue is a placeholder hotel in Udaipur.

It is the most literal Mehendi card in the set, so guests know what the gathering is before they read a word. That makes it a good fit for a mixed guest list, in India or abroad, where the English wording does the explaining. Mehendi is usually centred on the bride, so remove the groom's name if he will not be there, and say how long it runs if guests can come and go. Compared with Emerald Henna Courtyard, also English with no religious line, Mehendi Hands is warmer and simpler: Henna Courtyard has emerald green, gold arches and a half mandala instead of hands. For a Hindi card, look at Jharokha Jaal.
